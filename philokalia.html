The Philokalia

“Watchfulness is the mind firmly planted and standing at the door of the heart.”Saint Hesychius of Jerusalem, in the Philokalia

“Philokalia” means “love of the beautiful”. It is the name of an anthology of the Church Fathers, from the fourth to the fifteenth century, on inner watchfulness, guarding the heart and the Jesus Prayer. The Russian pilgrim carried it in his bag; Saint Seraphim read it in his forest. The Philokalia

01 What is the Philokalia?

Its full title is The Philokalia of the Holy Neptic Fathers: the “love of the beautiful” gathered from the “watchful” Fathers. It is a collection of texts by more than thirty authors, most of them monks, written in Greek between the fourth and the fifteenth century. They do not speak of abstract theology but of experience: how to purify the heart of the passions, how to guard one’s thoughts, how to pray without ceasing.

Three words to enter the Philokalia

  • Watchfulness (nepsis, “sobriety”): staying inwardly awake, not letting oneself be carried away by thoughts.
  • Guarding the heart: noticing thoughts as soon as they approach, and rejecting those that lead to sin.
  • The Jesus Prayer: the constant, humble calling on the Lord’s name, which gathers the mind into the heart.

In The Way of a Pilgrim, the elder explains the Philokalia with an image: Holy Scripture is the dazzling sun, and the Philokalia is the glass through which one can look at it without being blinded. It is not “above” the Bible: it sheds light on what is hidden in it.

02 The story of a book

  1. 4th–15th c.

    Fathers of the desert and of Athos

    Monks of Egypt, Sinai, Constantinople and Mount Athos write about prayer and the inner struggle. Their texts circulate in manuscripts from monastery to monastery.

  2. 1782

    The Greek Philokalia

    Saint Nikodemos the Hagiorite and Saint Makarios of Corinth gather these texts and publish them in Venice.

  3. 1793

    The Slavonic Dobrotolubiye

    Saint Paisius Velichkovsky, who worked on the manuscripts for twenty-five years, publishes a Slavonic translation in Moscow. Saint Seraphim very probably read it, and it nourished the elders of Optina.

  4. 1877–1890

    The Russian Philokalia

    Saint Theophan the Recluse translates it into Russian in five volumes, adding many more texts.

  5. 20th c.

    In the West

    Translated into English, French and many other languages, it introduces the prayer of the heart far beyond the Orthodox world.

03 The Fathers of the Philokalia

Here are some of the most widely read authors, in chronological order:

  • Evagrius of Pontus (4th c.): the great observer of thoughts and passions.
  • Saint Mark the Ascetic and Saint Diadochos of Photiki (5th c.): the grace received in Baptism, and discernment.
  • Saint Hesychius and Saint Philotheus of Sinai: watchfulness and guarding the heart.
  • Saint Maximus the Confessor (7th c.): the Centuries on Love.
  • Saint Symeon the New Theologian (10th–11th c.): the experience of the divine light.
  • Nikephoros the Monk, Saint Gregory of Sinai and Saint Gregory Palamas (13th–14th c.): the prayer of the heart and the defence of the hesychasts.
  • Kallistos and Ignatius Xanthopoulos (14th c.): a complete teaching in a hundred chapters.

04 Selected passages

These are the passages that The Way of a Pilgrim offers, in its “three keys”, for entering into inner prayer. We translate them from the Russian text it quotes.

Watchfulness is the mind firmly planted and standing at the door of the heart, so that it sees the alien thoughts approaching, those thieves, hears what these destroyers say and do, and what image the demons are sketching in order to lure the mind into fantasy and deceive it.Saint Hesychius of Jerusalem

Saint Hesychius sums up the inner struggle in four activities, to be kept at every moment: humility, for only Christ can help us; attention, to keep the heart free of thoughts; immediate rejection of the evil thought; and prayer, to cry out to Christ from the depth of the heart.

Watchfulness is rightly called a path, for it leads to the Kingdom, both the one within us and the one to come.Saint Philotheus of Sinai
A footprint in the snow melts when the sun rises or is washed away by seeping water. So too the memories imprinted on the mind by acts of pleasure are wiped out by Christ rising in the heart through prayer, and by the rain of tears of compunction.Saint Theoliptos of Philadelphia
The work of your heart must be to pray to God without ceasing. Do you wish to succeed in it? Make a beginning and seek without laziness, in hope, and God will bless you with success.Saints Barsanuphius and John of Gaza
Man is like a tree: bodily labour is the leaves, and guarding the heart is the fruit.Abba Agathon, quoted by Nikephoros the Monk

05 How to read it today

The Philokalia was written by monks, for monks guided by a spiritual father. Yet Saint Theophan translated it for all Christians: it is solid food for anyone who reads it with humility and discernment.

  • Start with the simplest. The Way of a Pilgrim is the best introduction. There are also selections, such as Writings from the Philokalia on Prayer of the Heart.
  • Read little, but slowly. One chapter, a few lines, re-read and pondered, are worth more than pages skimmed.
  • Look for practice, not technique. What matters is humility, guarding one’s thoughts and prayer. The bodily methods described by some authors are secondary.
  • Read within the Church. Talk to your confessor about your reading, and live it with the sacraments.

The Fathers give a warning. Several texts in the Philokalia describe very high spiritual states. Reading about them is not living them: trying to reproduce them on one’s own, without guidance, leads to spiritual delusion (prelest). The first fruit of the Philokalia is humility.

Frequently asked questions

The Philokalia in questions

The Greek word means “love of the beautiful” or “love of the good”. In Slavonic and Russian it is Добротолюбие (Dobrotolubiye), with the same meaning. The beauty in question is that of the purified soul, in which the image of God shines again.

Yes. Its compilers, and later Saint Theophan, meant it to serve all Christians, and unceasing prayer is asked of everyone. But it is better to start with selected texts and to talk with a priest, rather than reading all the volumes alone.

To begin, a selection such as Writings from the Philokalia on Prayer of the Heart. To go further, the complete English translation by G. E. H. Palmer, Philip Sherrard and Kallistos Ware. In Russian, Saint Theophan’s Philokalia can be read freely on azbyka.ru. The parish priest can advise you.
