Saint John of Kronstadt

“Prayer is the breath of the soul.”Saint John of Kronstadt, My Life in Christ

For more than fifty years he was the priest of a single parish, St Andrew’s Cathedral in Kronstadt, near Saint Petersburg. All Russia came to him. A friend of the poor and a man of ardent prayer, he left a famous spiritual diary: My Life in Christ.Priest in Kronstadt for over fifty years, friend of the poor and teacher of prayer.

Saint John of Kronstadt: life and teaching

01 A life in dates

John Ilyich Sergiev was born into a very poor family in the Russian far north: his father was a church reader in the village. A sickly child, he struggled to learn to read, until one day, after fervent prayer, everything became clear to him.

  1. 1829

    Birth

    On 19 October, in the village of Sura, Arkhangelsk province.

  2. 1855

    Priest in Kronstadt

    A graduate of the Saint Petersburg Theological Academy, he marries Elizabeth Nesvitskaya and becomes a priest of St Andrew’s Cathedral in Kronstadt, where he will serve all his life.

  3. 1882

    The House of Industry

    He founds a “House of Industry” in Kronstadt: workshops, a school, a shelter and a soup kitchen, to help the poor live by their own work.

  4. 1908

    His repose

    He falls asleep in the Lord on 20 December (2 January 1909 in the civil calendar). He is buried at the St John Convent in Saint Petersburg, which he had founded.

  5. 1964

    Canonization

    The Russian Orthodox Church Outside of Russia proclaims him a saint; the Church in Russia does so in 1990.

02 Pastor of all Russia

In Kronstadt, a town of sailors and poor people, the young priest goes to the most wretched, shares all he has and prays at the bedside of the sick. Little by little his reputation grows: people come from all over Russia to confess, to ask for his prayers and to attend his Liturgy.

  • The Liturgy every day. He celebrates almost daily, with a fervour that deeply moves the faithful, and invites them to receive communion often.
  • General confession. Faced with crowds — sometimes several thousand people — and unable to hear each confession, he holds a common confession aloud before communion.
  • The poor. He prefers to give work rather than humiliating alms: that is the meaning of his House of Industry.
  • The sick. He travels through Saint Petersburg and Russia visiting the sick; many healings are attributed to his prayers.

The young Symeon, the future Saint Silouan the Athonite, left him a note before leaving for Mount Athos: “Father, I want to become a monk; pray that the world does not hold me back.”

03 My Life in Christ

For years Father John wrote down his thoughts, struggles and prayers every day. Published in his lifetime as My Life in Christ, this spiritual diary has been translated into many languages. Here are a few lines:

Prayer is the breath of the soul; prayer is our spiritual food and drink.My Life in Christ
Prayer is the lifting up of the mind and heart to God.My Life in Christ
Do not confuse the person, this image of God, with the evil that is in him: evil is only his accidental misfortune, a sickness, a demonic dream; but his essence, the image of God, still remains in him.My Life in Christ
Love calms the heart and pleasantly enlarges it, it brings it to life; hatred painfully constricts and troubles it.My Life in Christ

04 Praying like Saint John

Saint John of Kronstadt insists on attentive and sincere prayer: the heart must say what the lips say. He teaches us to pray with faith, with the assurance that God hears, and to come often to the sacraments, above all to Holy Communion.

His advice

  • Keep heart and lips in harmony: when saying the Lord’s Prayer, ask yourself whether you really live what you say.
  • Never despair: after a sin, turn to God at once with trust.
  • See the image of God in everyone, even in those who do us harm.
  • Live from the Eucharist, the heart of all Christian life.
