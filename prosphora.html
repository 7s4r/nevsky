Prosphora, the bread of offering

01 What is prosphora?

The Greek word prosphora means “offering”. It is a small round leavened loaf made only of wheat flour, water, leaven and a little salt, often with holy water. It is baked with prayer by someone blessed for this service, the prosphornitsa.

A bread that speaks

  • Two layers, shaped separately and then joined: they recall the two natures of Christ, divine and human, and also the human person, body and soul.
  • The seal on top: a cross with the letters IC XC NI KA, “Jesus Christ conquers”.
  • The leaven recalls the life-giving power of the Holy Spirit; the flour of many grains, humanity gathered into one body.

02 The bread of offering

In the Church of the first centuries, the faithful themselves brought the bread, wine, oil and wax needed for the services. The deacons received these offerings and wrote down the names of those who had brought them; these names were read out during the Liturgy. Offerings were also made in the name of the departed.

Part of the bread and wine was consecrated for the Eucharist; the rest was shared at a fraternal meal, the agape. Gradually the word “prosphora” came to mean only the bread of the Liturgy, baked especially for it. The link between the bread offered and the names of those we commend to God has remained: that is the whole meaning of the “notes” given in today.

03 At the Liturgy: the five loaves

Before the Liturgy, the priest prepares the gifts at the table of oblation: this is the proskomedia. He uses five prosphoras, in memory of the five loaves of the feeding of the multitude (Matthew 14:17–21).

  1. 1st

    The Lamb

    With a small lance the priest cuts out the square centre marked IC XC NI KA, saying the words of Isaiah: “He was led as a sheep to the slaughter.” This bread, the Lamb, will become the Body of Christ.

  2. 2nd

    The Mother of God

    A triangular particle is placed at the right of the Lamb: “At thy right hand stood the Queen” (Psalm 44/45).

  3. 3rd

    The saints

    Nine particles are taken for the “nine ranks” of saints: St John the Baptist, the prophets, the apostles, the hierarchs, the martyrs, the monastic saints, the unmercenaries, the saints of the day and the author of the Liturgy.

  4. 4th

    The living

    The priest takes particles for the Church, the patriarch, the bishop and the living whom he names.

  5. 5th

    The departed

    Then for the departed: our parents, our loved ones, all who rest in the hope of the resurrection.

All these particles are arranged on the diskos around the Lamb: an icon of the whole Church — the Mother of God, the saints, the living and the departed — gathered around Christ.

04 Our loved ones in the prosphora

Before the Liturgy, the faithful hand in notes with the baptismal names of their loved ones: one note “for the health” of the living, another “for the repose” of the departed. The names are read at the altar, and for each one the priest takes a particle from a prosphora. That prosphora is then given back to you after the service.

At the end of the Liturgy, after the communion of the faithful, all the particles are placed in the Chalice while the priest says:

Wash away, O Lord, the sins of those here commemorated, by thy precious Blood, through the prayers of thy saints.Divine Liturgy

That is why the Church values prayer for one another so highly: our loved ones, living and departed, are presented to Christ in the very sacrifice of the Liturgy.

05 Antidoron and artos

  • Antidoron (“instead of the gift”): the pieces of the prosphora from which the Lamb was taken. They are given out at the end of the Liturgy, when the faithful come to venerate the cross. St Symeon of Thessalonica explains that it is given “instead of the dread Gifts” to those who have not received communion.
  • Artos (“leavened bread”): a large loaf blessed on Pascha, marked with the cross or the icon of the Resurrection. It remains in the church throughout Bright Week, then is broken and shared among the faithful, who keep it with reverence.

This is not Communion. Prosphora, antidoron and artos are blessed bread. Only the consecrated Gifts in the Chalice are the Body and Blood of Christ.

06 At home

  • Fasting, with prayer. Prosphora is eaten in the morning, before any other food, often with a little holy water, after making the sign of the cross.
  • In small pieces. To keep it, cut it into small pieces while fresh and let them dry in a cloth bag; it will then keep for a long time.
  • Near the icons. Keep it with reverence in your icon corner.
  • If it spoils, do not throw it away: burn it, or take it back to the church.

07 Prosphora seals

The seal that marks the prosphora is often a beautiful object, made of wood, bronze or stone. Old seals are kept in museums as witnesses to the piety of generations of Christians.

Frequently asked questions

Prosphora in questions

Before the Liturgy, write the baptismal (Orthodox) names of your loved ones on a note — one for the living, another for the departed — and hand it in with an offering. Ask the parish how this is done; the church is currently closed for renovation.

Prosphora and antidoron are intended first of all for the faithful. In many parishes, blessed bread is also offered to those who come to venerate the cross at the end of the service. If in doubt, simply ask the priest.

Prosphoras for the Liturgy are baked with prayer, following precise rules, by people who have received a blessing to do so. If you would like to help the parish in this way, speak to the priest.

Because it connects three things: the offering of our hands, the sacrifice of Christ and the prayer of the whole Church. In receiving prosphora we take home a little of the Liturgy and of the prayer made for our loved ones.
