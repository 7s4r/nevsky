Saint John Chrysostom

“Glory to God for all things!”The last words of Saint John Chrysostom

Preacher in Antioch, Archbishop of Constantinople, dead in exile: the Church gave him the name “Chrysostom”, the “Golden Mouth”. Almost every Sunday we celebrate the Divine Liturgy that bears his name, and every Paschal night we read his homily. We keep his feast on 26 November (13 November in the Julian calendar).Preacher of Antioch, Archbishop of Constantinople, the “Golden Mouth” of the Church.

Saint John Chrysostom: life and teaching

01 A life in a few dates

John was born around 347 in Antioch, Syria, one of the great cities of the Roman Empire, into a noble family. His father, Secundus, a high-ranking officer of the imperial army, died early; his mother Anthusa, widowed at twenty, refused to remarry so as to devote herself to her son’s upbringing. He received the full Greek education of his day and learned eloquence from the pagan rhetorician Libanius. On his deathbed, when Libanius was asked whom he wanted as his successor, he answered: “John, had the Christians not stolen him from us.”

  1. c. 347

    Birth

    In Antioch, Syria, into a noble Christian family.

  2. c. 368

    Baptism

    Though born into a Christian family, he is only baptized as an adult, at about twenty, by Saint Meletius of Antioch, as was then common. Yet in his writings he defends the baptism of infants in Christian families.

  3. c. 374

    The desert

    After his mother’s death he lives as a monk in the mountains near Antioch, first under an elder, then alone in a cave. About four years of very harsh asceticism ruin his health for life: he suffers from his stomach until his death.

  4. 386

    Priest in Antioch

    Made deacon on his return, he is ordained priest at around forty and entrusted with preaching the Word of God. For twelve years he preaches almost daily; most of his homilies date from this time.

  5. 398

    Archbishop of Constantinople

    Brought almost by force to the capital, he finds a lax clergy, bribery, lavish banquets and huge expenses. He tackles these abuses and makes many enemies.

  6. 403

    The “Synod of the Oak”

    A party forms against him around Empress Eudoxia, whom he rebukes publicly, and Theophilus, Archbishop of Alexandria, who wants to subject the Church of Constantinople to Egypt. A robber council deposes him; he is exiled, recalled, then exiled again in 404 to Cucusus in Armenia.

  7. 407

    His repose

    Driven harshly towards Pityus on the Black Sea (today Pitsunda, in Abkhazia), he stops exhausted at Comana in Pontus. The holy martyr Basiliscus, buried nearby, appears to him and foretells his end. On 14 September he receives Holy Communion and gives up his soul to God, saying: “Glory to God for all things!”

  8. 438

    The return

    On 27 January, Emperor Theodosius II brings his relics back to Constantinople and asks forgiveness for his parents.

  9. 1204

    In Rome

    During the Fourth Crusade, his relics are taken to Rome and placed in a chapel of St Peter’s Basilica.

  10. 2004

    Back to Constantinople

    Pope John Paul II returns the relics of Saint John Chrysostom and Saint Gregory the Theologian to the Patriarchate of Constantinople.

02 The Golden Mouth

Saint John received the name “Chrysostom”, “Golden Mouth”, from the crowds who flocked to hear him: there was no other word for the beauty and power of his preaching. He often preached every day, without notes, expounding Scripture book by book. More than seven hundred of his homilies survive, on Genesis, the Psalms, the Gospels of Matthew and John and all the epistles of Saint Paul, whom he loved above all. He knew the Bible so well that his sermons contain some seven thousand quotations from the Old Testament and eleven thousand from the New.

Trained in the theological school of Antioch, a pupil of Diodore of Tarsus and an acquaintance of Theodore of Mopsuestia, he avoided the excesses of that school and remains a model of Orthodox thought for the whole Church.

  • Scripture for everyone. He urges the faithful to read the Bible at home, as a family, and to talk over Sunday’s sermon together.
  • The poor. For him, Christ is best served by showing mercy to the poor, for it is Christ himself whom we help. As archbishop, he sells the costly furnishings of his residence, and even marble intended to adorn a church, to support a hospital and build several more.
  • The family. He calls the Christian home a “little church”, where people pray, read the Gospel and learn to love.
  • Courage. He flatters neither the rich nor the imperial court, and publicly rebukes Empress Eudoxia for her injustices. He pays for it with exile, and dies giving thanks: “Glory to God for all things!”

With Saint Basil the Great and Saint Gregory the Theologian, he is one of the Three Holy Hierarchs, the great teachers of the Church whom we celebrate together on 12 February (30 January). He is also venerated by Catholics, Anglicans, Lutherans and the non-Chalcedonian Oriental Churches.

04 The Liturgy and the Paschal homily

The Divine Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom is the one the Orthodox Church celebrates on almost every day of the year, and the one you will hear most often in our church. Saint John composed it on the basis of the ancient anaphora known as “of the Twelve Apostles”, used in Antioch; according to tradition, he also made its prayers shorter than those of Saint Basil so that the faithful would not grow weary of coming to pray. It became the most widely used liturgy of the Byzantine rite, and at the end of every Liturgy the priest commemorates “our father among the saints John Chrysostom, Archbishop of Constantinople”. The Liturgy of Saint Basil is now celebrated only ten times a year, including the Sundays of Great Lent.

One prayer of this Liturgy, said during the antiphons, sums up the spirit of common prayer:

You have given us grace to make these common prayers with one accord, and have promised that when two or three are gathered together in your name you will grant their requests: fulfil now the requests of your servants for their good.Prayer of the third antiphon

Every year, at Matins on Paschal night, the priest reads aloud the Paschal homily of Saint John Chrysostom. All are invited to rejoice, those who have fasted and those who have not, the labourers of the first hour and those of the eleventh:

Let no one lament their poverty, for the universal Kingdom has been revealed. Let no one mourn their transgressions, for forgiveness has dawned from the tomb. Let no one fear death, for the Saviour’s death has set us free. […] Christ is risen, and life reigns!Paschal homily

05 Praying with Saint John

Among the evening prayers, the Russian prayer book contains the twenty-four prayers of Saint John Chrysostom, one for each hour of the day and night. They are very short petitions that can be said slowly, one at a time:

O Lord, deprive me not of your heavenly blessings. O Lord, deliver me from eternal torment. […] O Lord, grant me tears, remembrance of death and compunction. […] O Lord, grant me to love you with all my soul and mind, and to do your will in all things.Prayers of Saint John Chrysostom for the twenty-four hours

Several prayers before Holy Communion and in the sacrament of Holy Unction also come from him, including this one: “I believe, O Lord, and I confess that you are truly the Christ, the Son of the living God, who came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am the first.”

The troparion of Saint John (tone 8)

Grace shining forth from your mouth like a torch has enlightened the universe; it has laid up for the world treasures of detachment and shown us the height of humility. Teaching us by your words, O Father John Chrysostom, pray to the Word, Christ our God, to save our souls.

Frequently asked questions

Saint John Chrysostom in questions

On 26 November (13 November in the Julian calendar). He died on 14 September, the feast of the Exaltation of the Cross; so that his memory might be honoured on its own, the Church set it on 13 November, the day of his enthronement in Constantinople. The return of his relics to Constantinople is kept on 9 February (27 January), and the Three Holy Hierarchs on 12 February (30 January).

It is a Greek word meaning “Golden Mouth”, the name the people gave him in wonder at his eloquence. In Russian he is called Иоанн Златоуст, which means the same.

Yes. The Divine Liturgy is the heart of the Church’s life, the celebration of the Eucharist. The form that bears the name of Saint John Chrysostom is celebrated on most Sundays and feast days, in our church as throughout the Orthodox Church.

Brought back to Constantinople in 438, his relics were taken to Rome during the Fourth Crusade in 1204 and placed in a chapel of St Peter’s Basilica. In 2004 Pope John Paul II returned them, together with those of Saint Gregory the Theologian, to the Patriarchate of Constantinople, where they are venerated in the patriarchal Church of Saint George. His head is kept in Florence, in the cathedral of Santa Maria del Fiore; other portions are on Mount Athos and in Russia.
