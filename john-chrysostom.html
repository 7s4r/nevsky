Saint John Chrysostom

“Glory to God for all things!”The last words of Saint John Chrysostom

Preacher in Antioch, Archbishop of Constantinople, dead in exile: the Church gave him the name “Chrysostom”, the “Golden Mouth”. Almost every Sunday we celebrate the Divine Liturgy that bears his name, and every Paschal night we read his homily. We keep his feast on 26 November (13 November in the Julian calendar).Preacher of Antioch, Archbishop of Constantinople, the “Golden Mouth” of the Church.

Saint John Chrysostom: life and teaching

01 A life in a few dates

John was born around 347 in Antioch, one of the great cities of the Roman Empire. His father, an army officer, died soon after his birth; his mother Anthusa, widowed at twenty, refused to remarry so as to devote herself to her son’s upbringing. He studied rhetoric under the famous Libanius, who would have made him his successor “had the Christians not stolen him from us”.

  1. c. 347

    Birth

    In Antioch, Syria, into a well-to-do Christian family.

  2. c. 368

    Baptism

    Baptized by Saint Meletius, Bishop of Antioch, he becomes a reader and gives himself to the study of Scripture.

  3. c. 372

    The desert

    He lives as an ascetic in the mountains near Antioch, first under an elder, then alone in a cave, until his health forces him back to the city.

  4. 386

    Priest in Antioch

    Ordained priest, he preaches for twelve years in the great church of Antioch. Crowds flock to hear him.

  5. 398

    Archbishop of Constantinople

    Brought almost by force to the imperial capital, he reforms the clergy, founds hospitals and denounces the luxury of the powerful.

  6. 404

    Exile

    Condemned by a council of his opponents and at odds with Empress Eudoxia, he is banished to Cucusus in Armenia.

  7. 407

    His repose

    On 14 September, exhausted by a forced march to a more distant exile, he falls asleep in the Lord at Comana, on the Black Sea.

  8. 438

    The return

    On 27 January, Emperor Theodosius II brings his relics back to Constantinople and asks forgiveness for his parents.

02 The Golden Mouth

Saint John only received the name “Chrysostom” after his death: there was no other word for the beauty and power of his preaching. He often preached several times a week, without notes, expounding Scripture book by book. More than seven hundred of his homilies survive, on Genesis, the Psalms, the Gospels of Matthew and John and all the epistles of Saint Paul, whom he loved above all.

  • Scripture for everyone. He urges the faithful to read the Bible at home, as a family, and to talk over Sunday’s sermon together.
  • The poor. He never tires of saying that the poor man is Christ himself, and that almsgiving is worth more than the gold of the sacred vessels. As archbishop, he sells the costly furnishings of his residence and opens hospitals.
  • The family. He calls the Christian home a “little church”, where people pray, read the Gospel and learn to love.
  • Courage. He flatters neither the rich nor the imperial court, and pays for it with exile. He dies giving thanks: “Glory to God for all things!”

With Saint Basil the Great and Saint Gregory the Theologian, he is one of the Three Holy Hierarchs, the great teachers of the Church whom we celebrate together on 12 February (30 January).

04 The Liturgy and the Paschal homily

The Divine Liturgy of Saint John Chrysostom is the one the Orthodox Church celebrates on almost every day of the year, and the one you will hear most often in our church. According to tradition, Saint John shortened the prayers of the older Liturgy of Saint Basil so that the faithful would not grow weary of coming to pray. The Liturgy of Saint Basil is now celebrated only ten times a year, including the Sundays of Great Lent.

One prayer of this Liturgy, said during the antiphons, sums up the spirit of common prayer:

You have given us grace to make these common prayers with one accord, and have promised that when two or three are gathered together in your name you will grant their requests: fulfil now the requests of your servants for their good.Prayer of the third antiphon

Every year, at Matins on Paschal night, the priest reads aloud the Paschal homily of Saint John Chrysostom. All are invited to rejoice, those who have fasted and those who have not, the labourers of the first hour and those of the eleventh:

Let no one lament their poverty, for the universal Kingdom has been revealed. Let no one mourn their transgressions, for forgiveness has dawned from the tomb. Let no one fear death, for the Saviour’s death has set us free. […] Christ is risen, and life reigns!Paschal homily

05 Praying with Saint John

Among the evening prayers, the Russian prayer book contains the twenty-four prayers of Saint John Chrysostom, one for each hour of the day and night. They are very short petitions that can be said slowly, one at a time:

O Lord, deprive me not of your heavenly blessings. O Lord, deliver me from eternal torment. […] O Lord, grant me tears, remembrance of death and compunction. […] O Lord, grant me to love you with all my soul and mind, and to do your will in all things.Prayers of Saint John Chrysostom for the twenty-four hours

The prayer said before Holy Communion is also attributed to him: “I believe, O Lord, and I confess that you are truly the Christ, the Son of the living God, who came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am the first.”

The troparion of Saint John (tone 8)

Grace shining forth from your mouth like a torch has enlightened the universe; it has laid up for the world treasures of detachment and shown us the height of humility. Teaching us by your words, O Father John Chrysostom, pray to the Word, Christ our God, to save our souls.
