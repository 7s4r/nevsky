Blessings, prayer for the sick and funerals

01 Blessing a home

It is traditional to have one’s home blessed when moving in, and then every year after Theophany, with the holy water of the feast. The priest comes to your home, prays for those who live there, sprinkles the rooms with holy water and traces crosses of holy oil on the walls.

  • To prepare: a small table with an icon, a candle, a bowl of water (or holy water you already have), and the list of the baptismal names of the household.
  • To ask: agree on a date with the priest through the contact form.

02 Blessing objects

The priest can bless an icon, a cross, a prayer rope, and also a car, a workplace or a garden: all creation is called to be sanctified. Bring the object to church and ask the priest before or after a service.

03 Prayer for the sick

  • The moleben: a service of intercession for the health of a sick person, which can be requested at church. You can also give the sick person’s name to be commemorated at the Liturgy (commemoration notes).
  • Communion at home: the priest can bring communion to the sick and the elderly who cannot come to church, at home or in hospital.
  • Holy Unction (anointing of the sick): a sacrament of healing of soul and body. The priest reads seven epistles and seven gospels and anoints the sick person seven times with blessed oil (James 5:14-15). It is celebrated for a sick person, or for all the faithful during Great Lent.

04 Funerals

When an Orthodox relative dies, let the priest know as soon as possible, before fixing the date with the funeral directors: in France, funerals must take place within six days. The priest can come and pray beside the deceased straight away.

  1. Before the service, the deceased is prepared: they wear their baptismal cross; a band decorated with icons is placed on the forehead, and an icon or a cross in the hands.
  2. The funeral service in church, with lighted candles: psalms, canon, readings from the Epistle and the Gospel, then the prayer of absolution, whose text is placed in the hand of the deceased.
  3. The last farewell: everyone comes to kiss the icon or the cross, while “Come, let us give the last kiss…” is sung.
  4. The burial, to the singing of “Memory eternal”. The Orthodox Church favours burial; if cremation is being considered, please speak to the priest.

The funeral service is celebrated for departed who were baptized Orthodox. For a relative of another confession, the priest can pray with the family; ask him what is possible.

05 Prayer for the departed

The Church never ceases to pray for those who have fallen asleep. A panikhida (memorial service) is served on the 3rd, 9th and 40th day after death, and then on each anniversary. The name of the departed can be given for the Liturgy (commemoration notes).

On the Saturdays of the departed, shown in the parish calendar, the whole Church prays for the departed.

In short

  • Bless your home when you move in, then after Theophany.
  • Objects are blessed at church, before or after a service.
  • For the sick: moleben, communion at home, Holy Unction.
  • When someone dies, tell the priest before fixing the funeral date.
  • Pray for the departed on the 3rd, 9th and 40th day, and every year.
