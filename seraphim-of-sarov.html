Saint Seraphim of Sarov

“My joy, Christ is risen!”How Saint Seraphim greeted everyone who came to him

Monk, hermit in the forest, recluse, and then an elder whom all Russia came to consult, Saint Seraphim of Sarov is one of the best-loved Russian saints. In his conversation with Nicholas Motovilov he summed up the Gospel in one sentence: the aim of the Christian life is the acquisition of the Holy Spirit.Hermit, elder and one of the best-loved Russian saints: the aim of the Christian life is the acquisition of the Holy Spirit.

Saint Seraphim of Sarov

01 A life in dates

Prokhor Moshnin was born in Kursk into a devout merchant family. As a child he fell from the top of a church bell tower under construction and got up unharmed; later, when he was gravely ill, he was healed as the wonderworking Kursk icon of the Mother of God was carried past, as the Virgin had promised him in a dream. At nineteen he entered the monastery of Sarov, deep in the forest, where he would live for more than fifty years.

  1. 1754

    Born in Kursk

    The early Lives give 1759. The Kursk parish registers, published in 1903, point to 1754.

  2. 1778

    Novice at Sarov

    Received into the monastery on 20 November. Eight years of obediences: in the bakery, the carpentry shop, as sacristan…

  3. 1786

    The monk Seraphim

    Tonsured a monk with the name Seraphim, “the fiery one”. Ordained deacon in 1787 and priest in 1793.

  4. 1794

    The hermitage

    On 20 November he goes to live alone in a hut in the forest, a few miles from the monastery.

  5. 1810

    Seclusion

    Back in the monastery, he lives in seclusion in his cell: five years completely, then ten more years with his door open to visitors.

  6. 1825

    The elder

    He leaves seclusion. Crowds come to him. He guides the sisters of Diveyevo.

  7. 1831

    The conversation with Motovilov

    In November, in the snowy forest, he explains the aim of the Christian life to young Nicholas Motovilov.

  8. 1833

    His repose

    On 2 January (15 January in the civil calendar) he is found dead, kneeling before the icon of the Mother of God “of Tenderness”.

  9. 1903

    Glorification

    Canonized on 19 July (1 August), in the presence of the imperial family and hundreds of thousands of pilgrims.

  10. 1991

    The relics return

    Lost during the Soviet period, his relics are found again and brought to Diveyevo Convent, where they rest today.

02 The hermit of the forest

In his “far hermitage” Father Seraphim tends a vegetable garden, chops wood, reads the Gospel and the Philokalia, and follows in spirit, day after day, the whole cycle of the Church’s services. He fasts strictly: for nearly three years he lives on nothing but a wild herb, goutweed.

  • A thousand days and nights on a rock. In imitation of Saint Simeon the Stylite, he prays standing on a rock in the forest by night and on another in his cell by day, repeating, according to tradition, the prayer of the publican: “God be merciful to me a sinner”. Only later did he tell a few of the brethren about it.
  • Peace with creation. The animals of the forest came to him. Witnesses saw him feed a bear from his hand: it is one of the best-known images of his life.
  • Forgiveness. On 12 September 1804, three peasants from nearby villages attack him while he is chopping wood, convinced he has money. He does not defend himself. They beat him with the axe handle and logs, leave him for dead and find nothing. He remains bent for the rest of his life. When the culprits are found, he begs that nothing be done to them: “send me away from the monastery rather than touch them”.
God be merciful to me a sinner.The prayer of the publican (Luke 18:13), which tradition says Saint Seraphim prayed on the rock

03 “My joy, Christ is risen!”

After fifteen years of seclusion, Father Seraphim opens his door to all. Nobles and peasants, the sick, anxious mothers, young people unsure of their way: every day a crowd waits at his door. Dressed in a poor white smock, he receives them with a father’s tenderness. He kisses them, anoints them with oil from the lamp before his icon, and greets each one, in every season, with the Paschal greeting: “Christ is risen!” He calls each of them “my joy”.

He had the gift of insight and often answered a question before it was asked. Many healings are attributed to his prayers. He devoted his last years to the sisters of Diveyevo, for whom he founded a community that is today one of the great convents of Russia.

What his visitors took away

  • Joy: sadness and despondency do not come from God; the Christian already lives by the Resurrection.
  • Gentleness: not a single reproach, but a love that makes one want to change one’s life.
  • Simplicity: practical advice suited to each person, often a short rule of prayer.

Above all, he advised everyone always to keep the remembrance of God:

Walking and sitting, working, standing in church before the service begins, coming in and going out, keep this unceasingly on your lips and in your heart. By calling on the name of God in this way you will find rest, you will attain purity of soul and body, and the Holy Spirit will come to dwell in you.Saint Seraphim’s advice to his visitors (Chronicle of Diveyevo)

04 The conversation with Motovilov: the aim of the Christian life

Nicholas Motovilov (1808–1879), a young landowner from the Simbirsk region, had just been healed through the elder’s prayers. On a grey, snowy Thursday in November 1831, Father Seraphim sat him down on a tree stump in a clearing near his hermitage, across from the Sarovka river, and answered the question Motovilov had been asking since childhood: what is the aim of the Christian life?

Prayer, fasting, vigils and all other Christian works, however good they may be in themselves, are not the aim of our Christian life, although they serve as the indispensable means of attaining it. The true aim of our Christian life is the acquisition of the Holy Spirit of God.Saint Seraphim to Motovilov, November 1831

“Acquiring”, he explains, is like gaining capital: worldly people seek to acquire money and honours; the Christian seeks to acquire the grace of the Spirit. Every good deed done for Christ’s sake brings it, but prayer most of all, “for it is always in our hands”.

Motovilov then asks how one can know that one is in the Spirit of God. The elder takes him firmly by the shoulders:

“We are both in the Spirit of God now, my son. Why don’t you look at me?”
“I cannot look, Father, because lightning is flashing from your eyes. Your face has become brighter than the sun, and my eyes ache.”The conversation with Motovilov

Motovilov sees the saint’s face as if in the middle of the noonday sun; the light shines on the snow all around. The elder asks him what he feels:

Peace“Such calm and peace in my soul that no words can express it.”
SweetnessAn extraordinary sweetness filling the heart and the whole body.
JoyAn extraordinary joy in all my heart.
WarmthIn the midst of the snow, which does not melt: “this warmth is not in the air but in ourselves”.

This, the saint says, is the Kingdom of God “within you” (Luke 17:21): “righteousness, and peace, and joy in the Holy Ghost” (Romans 14:17). The text of the conversation, found among Motovilov’s papers, was first published in 1903, the year of the canonization.

05 Inner peace

His Spiritual Instructions, written down by his disciples, keep returning to peace of soul: it is the sign of God’s presence and the treasure Christ left to his own (John 14:27).

Nothing contributes so much to the acquisition of inner peace as silence and, as far as possible, unceasing converse with oneself and rare converse with others.Saint Seraphim, Spiritual Instructions
  • Do not judge. “Peace of soul is preserved by not judging and by silence.”
  • Drive away sadness. “To preserve peace of soul, one must drive away despondency and strive to have a joyful spirit, not a sad one.”
  • Bear offences. Do not be troubled by the insults of others; hold back anger and guard mind and heart through attention.
  • Shine on others. “When a man attains inner peace, he can shed the light that enlightens the mind upon others.”

“Acquire a peaceful spirit, and thousands around you will be saved.” These are the most famous words attributed to Saint Seraphim. They are not found in his written Instructions and come to us through oral tradition, yet they faithfully sum up the last saying quoted above. On the other hand, be wary of the many “prophecies” circulating under his name: Church historians have shown that many were attributed to him later.

06 Saint Seraphim’s rule of prayer

To lay people too busy for long prayers, the elder gave a very simple rule. It has remained famous as “Saint Seraphim’s rule”:

  1. Morning

    On rising, before the icons

    The Lord’s Prayer three times, in honour of the Holy Trinity; “O Theotokos and Virgin, rejoice” three times; the Creed once.

  2. Until noon

    At work or on the road

    Say quietly: “Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner”. If others are around, only in the mind: “Lord, have mercy”.

  3. Before lunch

    At midday

    Repeat the morning rule.

  4. Afternoon

    Until evening

    Say quietly: “Most Holy Theotokos, save me, a sinner”. When alone: “Lord Jesus Christ, through the Theotokos, have mercy on me, a sinner”.

  5. Evening

    Before sleep

    Repeat the morning rule, then fall asleep protected by the sign of the cross.

The saint explained that by keeping to it one “can attain the measure of Christian perfection”, for these three prayers are the foundation of the faith: the Lord’s Prayer, given by the Lord himself as the model of all prayers; the Archangel’s greeting to the Virgin; and the Creed, which sums up all the saving dogmas. If even this short rule cannot be kept properly, it can be said while working, walking, even lying in bed. Those with more time can add readings from the Gospel and the Epistles, psalms, canons and akathists. As always, talk to your confessor about your rule of prayer.

Frequently asked questions

Saint Seraphim in questions

Twice a year: 15 January (2 January in the Julian calendar), the day of his repose, and 1 August (19 July in the Julian calendar), the anniversary of his glorification in 1903.

At the Holy Trinity–Saint Seraphim Convent of Diveyevo, in the Nizhny Novgorod region. Confiscated in the 1920s, they were found again at the end of the Soviet period and transferred there in 1991. Sarov itself, nearby, is a closed city.

A landowner from the Simbirsk region (1808–1879), healed through the saint’s prayers in 1831. He called himself “the servant of the Mother of God and of Seraphim” and devoted much of his life and fortune to Diveyevo Convent. His notes, kept by his widow, were published by Sergei Nilus in 1903.

Receiving and letting grow within us the grace of God, already given in Baptism and Chrismation. Good works, fasting and prayer are not “merits” to be stored up, but means of opening ourselves to the Spirit, who gradually transforms the whole person. This is what the Church calls deification.

The saint certainly had the gift of insight. But many texts circulating under his name, about Russia or the end times, actually go back to the reflections of Motovilov or other authors and were attributed to him later. The Church urges caution: it is better to hold to his certain teaching on prayer, inner peace and the acquisition of the Holy Spirit.

The Conversation with Motovilov (often published as On the Acquisition of the Holy Spirit) and the Spiritual Instructions are short and available in English, often together with a Life of the saint. The original Russian texts can be read freely on azbyka.ru. The parish priest can recommend an edition.
