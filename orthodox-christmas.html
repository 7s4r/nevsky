Orthodox Christmas on 7 January

01 Why 7 January?

The Russian Orthodox Church, like the Churches of Jerusalem, Serbia and Georgia and Mount Athos, keeps the Julian calendar, established by Julius Caesar, for its liturgical life. The civil, Gregorian calendar was adopted in the West from 1582.

Today the two calendars are 13 days apart, so 25 December on the Julian calendar falls on 7 January on the civil calendar. From 2101 the gap will be 14 days and Christmas will fall on 8 January.

Key points

  • All Christians celebrate the Nativity on 25 December: only the calendar differs.
  • Some Orthodox Churches (Greek, Romanian, Bulgarian…) follow a “Revised Julian” calendar and keep Christmas on 25 December civil, but Pascha on the same day as us.
  • All fixed feasts of our parish are shifted by 13 days: Theophany on 19 January, the Annunciation on 7 April, and so on. See the parish calendar.

02 God becomes man

At Christmas the Church celebrates not only the birth of a child, but the mystery of the Incarnation: the eternal Son of God truly becomes man, born of the Virgin Mary, to save mankind and make us partakers of the divine life.

And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, full of grace and truth.John 1:14

This is why the Nativity is one of the twelve great feasts of the year, prepared by forty days of fasting and followed by twelve days of celebration.

03 The icon of the feast

The icon of the Nativity tells the whole feast in a single image:

  • The black cave at the centre: the world in darkness, into which the Light comes.
  • The swaddled Child in a manger that looks like a tomb: the One who is born will die and rise again for us.
  • The ox and the ass, after the prophet Isaiah: “The ox knoweth his owner, and the ass his master’s crib” (Isaiah 1:3).
  • The Mother of God, lying on her bed.
  • St Joseph, pensive, to one side; beside him an old man, an image of the tempter sowing doubt.
  • Angels, shepherds and wise men, guided by the ray of the star: heaven and earth, the simple and the learned, come to worship.

04 Christmas Eve, 6 January

The eve of the feast is called Sochelnik in Russian, after sochivo, the dish eaten that evening: boiled wheat (or rice) with honey, poppy seeds and dried fruit.

  1. Morning

    The Royal Hours

    A service of readings from the Old Testament and the Gospel on the birth of Christ.

  2. Day

    Vespers and the Liturgy of St Basil

    This is a strict fast day: according to the Typikon one does not eat until these services are over, or, by popular custom, until the first star appears, in memory of the star of Bethlehem.

  3. Evening

    The sochivo meal

    A Lenten family supper, often after prayer before the icons and the singing of the troparion.

  4. Night

    The Vigil and the Nativity Liturgy

    The All-Night Vigil and the Divine Liturgy of St John Chrysostom, often celebrated during the night. After communion the fast is broken: the feast has come!

05 The Sviatki, from Christmas to Theophany

The twelve days after Christmas, from 7 to 17 January, are called the Sviatki, the “holy days”. There is no fasting, even on Wednesdays and Fridays. People visit one another, sing carols (koliadki) and care for the poor and the lonely.

  • 14 January: the Circumcision of the Lord and St Basil the Great; it is 1 January on the Julian calendar, hence the “Old New Year” kept in Russia.
  • 18 January: the eve of Theophany, a strict fast day.
  • 19 January: Theophany, the Baptism of the Lord, with the great blessing of the waters.

During the feast, people greet one another in Russian with “Христос рождается!” — “Славите!” (“Christ is born! — Glorify Him!”), or simply “С Рождеством Христовым!”, “Merry Christmas!”.

06 The hymns of the feast

The troparion and kontakion of the Nativity are sung at every service of the feast until 13 January. They can also be sung at home, before the icons and at the festive meal.

Troparion of the Nativity

Tone 4

Thy Nativity, O Christ our God, has shone upon the world the light of knowledge: for by it, those who worshipped the stars were taught by a star to adore Thee, the Sun of Righteousness, and to know Thee, the Dayspring from on high. O Lord, glory to Thee!

In Slavonic

Рождество Твое, Христе Боже наш, возсия мирови свет разума: в нем бо звездам служащии звездою учахуся Тебе кланятися, Солнцу правды, и Тебе ведети с высоты Востока. Господи, слава Тебе.

Kontakion of the Nativity

Tone 3

Today the Virgin gives birth to the One who is above all being, and the earth offers a cave to the Unapproachable; angels with shepherds give glory, the wise men journey with the star: for unto us is born a young Child, the pre-eternal God.

In Slavonic

Дева днесь Пресущественнаго раждает, и земля вертеп Неприступному приносит; Ангели с пастырьми славословят, волсви же со звездою путешествуют: нас бо ради родися Отроча младо, Превечный Бог.

Frequently asked questions

Orthodox Christmas: questions

Yes, all of them: the Russian Church on 25 December of the Julian calendar, which is 7 January civil; the Churches that follow the Revised Julian calendar, such as the Greek Church, on 25 December civil.

The civil New Year falls during the Nativity Fast: families may gather, but with moderation. Many parishes hold a service of thanksgiving for the new year. The “Old New Year”, on 14 January, falls during the Christmas season.

The tree and presents are welcome family customs. In Russia, “Grandfather Frost” (Ded Moroz) belongs rather to New Year. The heart of the feast remains the Liturgy and the joy of the Saviour’s birth.
