The Nativity Fast

01 Forty days before Christmas

The Nativity Fast lasts from 28 November to 6 January (15 November to 24 December on the Julian calendar). It begins the day after the feast of the Apostle Philip, which is why in Russian it is also called “Philip’s Fast”.

Known since the early centuries, it was fixed at forty days in the 12th century, after the forty-day fasts of Moses before receiving the Tables of the Law and of Christ in the desert. It is the last of the four great fasts of the Church year.

In brief

  • Dates: 28 November to 6 January.
  • What one avoids: meat, dairy, eggs; fish is allowed on certain days.
  • The aim: not to lose weight, but to make room for God and our neighbour.

02 What one eats

  1. 28 Nov – 1 Jan

    The usual fast

    No meat, dairy or eggs. Fish is allowed on Saturdays and Sundays, and on great feasts (the Entry of the Theotokos into the Temple on 4 December, St Nicholas on 19 December). Monday, Wednesday and Friday are the strictest days.

  2. 2 – 6 Jan

    The last days

    The fast becomes stricter: no fish, even on Saturdays and Sundays.

  3. 6 Jan

    Christmas Eve

    A strict fast until the end of the services, or until the first star, then the sochivo meal.

Fasting is not a rule to apply on one’s own. The rules of the Typikon were written for monasteries; for lay people, the sick, pregnant women, children, the elderly or those doing hard work, the measure is decided with the priest or spiritual father.

For the general rules of fasting, the four fasts and meal ideas, see our guide to Orthodox fasting.

03 Feasts during the fast

  1. 4 Dec

    The Entry of the Theotokos into the Temple

    One of the twelve great feasts. From this day the Christmas hymns begin: “Christ is born: glorify Him!”

  2. 6 Dec

    St Alexander Nevsky, patronal feast of our church

    The great feast of our parish. Discover St Alexander Nevsky.

  3. 13 Dec

    St Andrew, the First-Called Apostle

    According to Tradition, he preached the Gospel as far as the lands of the future Russia.

  4. 19 Dec

    St Nicholas the Wonderworker

    One of the best-loved saints, a model of mercy.

  5. Sundays

    The Forefathers and the Fathers of Christ

    The two Sundays before Christmas commemorate those who awaited the Messiah, from Abraham to Joseph.

  6. 2 Jan

    St John of Kronstadt

    The great Russian pastor, apostle of prayer and charity. Discover St John of Kronstadt.

04 More than a diet

Fasting only makes sense together with prayer and love of neighbour. The Fathers of the Church say so again and again:

True fasting is to be estranged from evil, to restrain the tongue, to abstain from anger, to put away lusts, slander, lying and perjury.St Basil the Great, Homily on Fasting
  • Pray more: morning and evening prayers, reading the Gospel, the Jesus Prayer.
  • Go to confession and communion during the fast, to welcome Christ with a cleansed heart.
  • Give: what you save can go to the poor.
  • Be reconciled, visit lonely people, turn down the noise of screens and distractions.
