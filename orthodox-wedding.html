Marriage in the Orthodox Church

01 The meaning of marriage

The Orthodox wedding is called the crowning. The spouses receive crowns, the sign of the glory with which God clothes them and of the witness they are called to bear for one another. Their union is an image of the union of Christ and the Church (Ephesians 5:32).

It is not only a commitment between two people: God himself unites the spouses and blesses them for a life of faithfulness, love and prayer, open to children.

02 Conditions

  • Civil marriage first. In France, a religious wedding may only take place after the civil marriage at the town hall: bring your livret de famille or marriage certificate.
  • Baptism. Both bride and groom are normally Orthodox. If one of them is a baptized Christian of another Church, the marriage is possible with the bishop’s blessing; speak to the priest early on.
  • Free consent of both, and no impediment (close kinship, a previous church marriage not dissolved, etc.).

03 Asking for the wedding and choosing the date

Get in touch with the priest several months in advance, through the contact form or after a service. He will meet you, go through the conditions and set the date.

In the Russian tradition, weddings are not celebrated during the four fasts, Cheesefare week, Bright Week after Easter and the period from Christmas to Theophany, nor on the eves of Wednesdays, Fridays, Sundays and great feasts. Possible days are therefore mainly Mondays, Wednesdays, Fridays and Sundays outside these periods. The parish calendar shows the fasts and feasts.

04 Preparing

  • Meetings with the priest about the meaning of Christian marriage, life as a couple and the service itself.
  • Confession and communion: the bride and groom go to confession and receive communion before the wedding, ideally at the Liturgy on the same day or the day before.
  • Witnesses: in Russian custom, two friends hold the crowns above the heads of the couple; preferably they are Orthodox.

05 What to bring

  • The wedding rings, given to the priest before the service.
  • Two icons, of Christ and of the Mother of God, with which the parents traditionally bless the couple.
  • An embroidered towel or white cloth on which the couple stand during the crowning.
  • Red wine for the common cup, and candles for the couple.

The crowns are provided by the church. The priest will confirm what you need to bring.

06 How the service unfolds

The service lasts about an hour and has two parts.

  1. The betrothal, at the entrance of the church: the priest blesses the couple, gives them lighted candles and exchanges the rings three times, the sign of their pledge to each other.
  2. Entering the church while Psalm 127 (128) is sung: “Blessed is every one that feareth the Lord…”. The priest asks each of them whether they marry freely and are not promised to another.
  3. The crowning: the priest places the crowns, saying “The servant of God N. is crowned unto the handmaid of God N., in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit”, then blesses them: “O Lord our God, crown them with glory and honour!”
  4. The readings from the Epistle (Ephesians 5:20-33) and the Gospel of the wedding at Cana (John 2:1-11), followed by the Lord’s Prayer.
  5. The common cup: the couple drink three times from the same cup of wine, the sign of the life they will now share, in joy and in hardship.
  6. The procession: the priest leads the couple three times around the lectern, singing “Rejoice, O Isaiah…”. These are their first steps together, following Christ.
  7. The removal of the crowns and the final blessing; the couple venerate the icons and receive everyone’s good wishes.

In short

  • Civil marriage first, then contact the priest several months ahead.
  • No weddings during the fasts and certain feast periods.
  • Confession and communion for the bride and groom.
  • Rings, two icons, a white towel, red wine and candles.
  • About an hour: betrothal, crowning, common cup and procession.
