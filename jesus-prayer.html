The Jesus Prayer

“Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner.”The prayer the Russian pilgrim learned to say without ceasing

A poor man with a withered arm hears Saint Paul’s words read in church one Sunday: “Pray without ceasing.” He sets out on the road to understand how this is possible. His story, first published in 1881, has become one of the most widely read books of Orthodox spirituality. It leads us into the Jesus Prayer, at the heart of the tradition of the Fathers.The story of a pilgrim who set out to learn how to “pray without ceasing”, and an introduction to the prayer of the heart.

The Jesus Prayer

01 What is the Jesus Prayer?

The Jesus Prayer is a very short prayer, repeated with attention and humility, calling on the name of the Lord. It was born among the monks of the Egyptian and Sinai deserts. The hesychasts of Mount Athos handed it down from generation to generation. Through Saint Paisius Velichkovsky and the elders of Optina, it became the treasure of the whole Russian Church.

English

Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner.

The short form the pilgrim uses: “Lord Jesus Christ, have mercy on me.”
Church Slavonic

Господи Иисусе Христе, Сыне Божий, помилуй мя грешнаго.

Gospodi Iisuse Khriste, Syne Bozhiy, pomiluy mya greshnago.
Greek

Κύριε Ἰησοῦ Χριστέ, Υἱὲ τοῦ Θεοῦ, ἐλέησόν με τὸν ἁμαρτωλόν.

Kyrie Iesou Christe, Huie tou Theou, eleeson me ton hamartolon.

Its words come from the Gospel. They are the words of the blind man of Jericho, who cried out, “Jesus, thou Son of David, have mercy on me” (Luke 18:38), and of the publican who, standing afar off, prayed, “God be merciful to me a sinner” (Luke 18:13). Saint Paul teaches that “at the name of Jesus every knee should bow” (Philippians 2:10).

A prayer that contains the whole Gospel

  • “Lord”: we confess that Christ is God.
  • “Jesus”, which means “God saves”: we call him by his name, as a living Person who is close to us.
  • “Christ, Son of God”: we confess the faith of the Apostle Peter (Matthew 16:16).
  • “Have mercy on me, a sinner”: we stand before him in humility, trusting in his mercy.
The holy Fathers say that the Jesus Prayer is a summary of the whole Gospel.The Way of a Pilgrim, second narrative

02 The pilgrim’s book

The Russian title, Откровенные рассказы странника духовному своему отцу, means “Candid Narratives of a Pilgrim to His Spiritual Father”. In English it is best known as The Way of a Pilgrim. The narrator tells his own life to his spiritual father, in a simple and lively language full of popular flavour.

By the grace of God I am a Christian man; by my deeds, a great sinner; by my calling, a homeless wanderer of the humblest birth, roaming from place to place. My worldly goods are a bag of dry bread on my back and the Holy Bible in my breast pocket. That is all.The opening lines of the book
  1. c. 1860

    A manuscript

    The text is written in the mid-nineteenth century and first circulates in handwritten copies. One copy is made on Mount Athos.

  2. 1881

    First edition

    Abbot Paisius, superior of the Cheremis Monastery of the Archangel Michael (diocese of Kazan), publishes the first four narratives. A fourth edition appears in Moscow as early as 1884.

  3. 1911

    The sequel

    Three further narratives, found among the papers of Saint Ambrose of Optina, are published. Complete editions bring together seven narratives and three “keys” drawn from the Fathers.

  4. 20th c.

    A worldwide classic

    Republished by the Russian emigration in Paris and translated into many languages, the book introduces the West to the prayer of the heart. In 1961 the American novelist J. D. Salinger made it the bedside book of his heroine in Franny and Zooey.

The author remains unknown. Several names have been suggested: Archimandrite Michael (Kozlov), the monk Arseny (Troepolsky), Saint Ambrose of Optina, and even Saint Theophan the Recluse. None of these attributions is certain. The story probably draws on the real experience of pilgrims of the time.

03 The pilgrim’s path

The first narrative is a true initiation. Step by step, the pilgrim moves from a question to experience.

  1. The question

    “Pray without ceasing”

    On the twenty-fourth Sunday after Pentecost, he hears the reading from Saint Paul at the Liturgy (1 Thessalonians 5:17). How can one pray without ceasing when one has to work for a living? Sermons speak about prayer, but no one explains how to pray like this.

  2. The search

    Looking for a teacher

    A devout landowner, then an abbot, give him good words but “did not explain”. He goes on his way, reading the Bible.

  3. The elder

    The meeting

    On the high road, an old monk takes him to his hermitage. He explains what unceasing prayer is and opens for him the Philokalia (in Russian, Dobrotolubiye), the collection of the Fathers’ writings on inner prayer.

  4. 3,000

    A rule, and obedience

    The pilgrim hires himself out for the summer to guard a vegetable garden and lives alone in a hut. The elder gives him a prayer rope and tells him to say the prayer 3,000 times a day, “neither adding nor taking away on your own initiative”.

  5. 6,000

    The habit

    After two difficult days, the prayer becomes easy. The elder doubles the rule. After a week, the pilgrim feels that something is missing as soon as he stops praying.

  6. 12,000

    The prayer says itself

    One morning it is the prayer itself that wakes him. The elder then frees him from counting: “now call on the name of Jesus Christ without counting”.

  7. The road

    Alone with the Philokalia

    The elder dies at the end of the summer. For two roubles the pilgrim buys an old, worn copy of the Philokalia, mends it and puts it in his bag with his Bible. He sets out again, across Siberia to Irkutsk, intending to reach Jerusalem.

Whether you stand or sit, walk or lie down, say without ceasing: “Lord Jesus Christ, have mercy on me”, not loudly and without hurrying.The elder to the pilgrim, first narrative

In the second part, the pilgrim never reaches Jerusalem. He returns to his spiritual father with a travelling companion, a professor. Together with a schema-monk and a priest they talk about prayer, then set off again, asking each other for their prayers.

04 From the lips to the heart

The book describes a gradual path that cannot be rushed.

  • Prayer of the lips. The prayer is said quietly and faithfully, without chasing after feelings. This is the time of effort and perseverance.
  • Prayer of the mind. The mind gathers itself in the words of the prayer and learns to “listen” to it instead of thinking about other things.
  • Prayer of the heart. While crossing Siberia, the pilgrim feels the prayer “begin to pass into the heart”: his heart seems to say the words with each of its beats. Later he joins the prayer to his breathing, as Saint Gregory of Sinai, Callistus and Ignatius teach: “Lord Jesus Christ” while breathing in, “have mercy on me” while breathing out.
Sit down alone and in silence. Bow your head, close your eyes, breathe gently, and in your imagination look into your heart. Bring your mind, that is, your thoughts, down from your head into your heart. As you breathe, say quietly with your lips, or in your mind alone: “Lord Jesus Christ, have mercy on me.” Try to put away other thoughts, be calm and patient, and repeat this often.Saint Symeon the New Theologian, read by the elder from the Philokalia

The Fathers give a warning. The elder asks the pilgrim to come and see him regularly “with a simple-hearted confession and openness, for without the guidance of a teacher it is difficult and fruitless to take up inner prayer on one’s own”. He also warns him that it is too early to reach “with immoderate zeal” for the prayer of the heart, “so as not to fall into spiritual greed”. Breathing methods are only a secondary aid: what matters is humility and attention to God.

05 The fruits of prayer

Little by little, prayer transforms the pilgrim’s whole life. He sometimes walks seventy versts or more in a day “without feeling that I am walking; I feel only that I am saying the prayer”. Cold, hunger and pain no longer have a hold on him, and he forgets insults at once. He notices that the fruits of the prayer of the heart appear in three ways:

In the spiritThe sweetness of God’s love, inner peace, purity of thought, the delightful remembrance of God.
In the sensesA pleasant warmth of the heart, lightness and vigour, insensibility to illness and sorrow.
In revelationsUnderstanding of Holy Scripture, knowledge of the meaning of creatures, detachment from vanities, certainty that God is near and loves us.
When I began to pray with the heart, everything around me appeared delightful: the trees, the grass, the birds, the earth, the air, the light. Everything seemed to tell me that it exists for man and bears witness to God’s love for man. Everything prayed, everything sang the glory of God.The Way of a Pilgrim, second narrative

Prayer also opens the pilgrim to others: everyone he meets seems to him “as dear as my own family”. The book is full of such encounters: peasants, a forester, a postmaster, officers, devout families, even robbers. It shows that the prayer of the heart is not only for monks.

06 Praying the Jesus Prayer today

Not all of us are called to say 12,000 prayers a day on the roads of Siberia. But the Jesus Prayer is offered to everyone, in the middle of ordinary life. Here are a few simple pieces of advice, in keeping with the teaching of the Fathers.

  • Ask a priest for his blessing. This is the book’s first lesson: the pilgrim only makes progress under the guidance of his elder. Talk to your confessor about your rule of prayer.
  • Start modestly. A few minutes in the morning and evening with your usual prayers, or one round of a prayer rope of 33, 50 or 100 knots. Regularity matters more than quantity.
  • Say it slowly and attentively. Keep your mind in the words, without hurrying and without looking for images or special feelings.
  • Come back to it during the day. While walking, waiting or working, when tempted or angry: a few invocations are enough to turn towards Christ.
  • Do not lose heart because of distractions. The pilgrim too knew “heaviness, laziness, boredom and overwhelming sleep”. Each time, gently return to the prayer.
  • Live it within the Church. The Jesus Prayer does not replace the Divine Liturgy, confession, Holy Communion or the reading of the Gospel. It carries them into every day.
