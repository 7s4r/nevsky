Saint Silouan the Athonite

“Keep your mind in hell, and despair not.”The words of Christ to Saint Silouan

A Russian peasant of enormous strength who became a monk on Mount Athos, simple and almost without schooling. He saw the living Christ, and all his life he bore witness: God is love, and whoever has known that love cannot but pray for the whole world, enemies included.A Russian peasant turned monk of Athos, witness of God’s love and of prayer for the whole world.

Saint Silouan the Athonite

01 A life in dates

Symeon Ivanovich Antonov was born in a village of the Tambov province, in the heart of peasant Russia. As a young man he was unusually strong and loved life, parties and drink; once, in a fight, he nearly killed a man with a single blow. But God’s call never left him.

  1. 1866

    Birth

    In the village of Shovskoye, Tambov province, into a peasant family.

  2. Youth

    The call

    A dream overwhelms him: a serpent enters him through his mouth, and a voice of unearthly sweetness says: “You swallowed a serpent in your dream and it disgusts you; so it grieves me to see what you are doing.”

  3. Service

    A soldier in Saint Petersburg

    He serves in a sapper battalion of the Imperial Guard. At the end of his service he leaves a note for Saint John of Kronstadt: “Father, I want to become a monk; pray that the world does not hold me back.”

  4. 1892

    Mount Athos

    In the autumn he enters the Russian Monastery of Saint Panteleimon, where he will live for forty-six years.

  5. 1896

    The monk Silouan

    Tonsured a monk with the name Silouan, then into the great schema in 1911. He works at the mill, on a dependency of the monastery outside Athos, and then as steward, in charge of the workmen.

  6. 1938

    His repose

    He falls asleep in the Lord on 24 September, at the Monastery of Saint Panteleimon.

  7. 1948

    His writings published

    His disciple, the future Archimandrite Sophrony (Sakharov), publishes his Life and writings, which go around the world.

  8. 1987

    Canonization

    The Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople proclaims him a saint; the Russian Church later adds him to its calendar.

02 “He saw the living Christ”

His first months in the monastery are a battle. The young novice prays the Jesus Prayer ceaselessly, hardly sleeping, but thoughts and demons assail him; he feels utterly abandoned and reaches despair: “You are implacable!” That same day, at Vespers, in the church of the Prophet Elijah by the mill, before the icon of the Saviour, he sees the living Christ. The fire of grace fills his heart and his whole body.

He could never forget that gaze, “inexpressibly meek, boundlessly loving, joyful”. All the rest of his life flows from it: he now knows, by experience, that God is love, and that this love is known through the Holy Spirit.

In my first year in the monastery my soul came to know the Lord through the Holy Spirit. The Lord loves us greatly; this I know from the Holy Spirit, whom the Lord gave me of his mercy alone. I am an old man, preparing for death, and I write the truth for the sake of the people.Saint Silouan, Writings

03 “Keep your mind in hell, and despair not”

For fifteen years grace visits him and then withdraws, and he goes through terrible struggles. One day, unable to pray with a pure mind, he sees his cell full of demons and asks the Lord what to do. In his soul he hears: “The proud always suffer from demons in this way.” — “Lord, teach me what I must do so that my soul may become humble.” And the answer comes in his heart: “Keep your mind in hell, and despair not.”

The Lord had pity on me and himself taught me how to humble myself: “Keep your mind in hell, and despair not.” By this the enemies are vanquished; but when my mind comes out of the fire, the thoughts gain strength again.Saint Silouan, Writings

How to understand these words

  • “Keep your mind in hell”: sincerely to see oneself as deserving separation from God, without comparing oneself with others or thinking oneself righteous. It is the way of humility, which pride cannot bear.
  • “And despair not”: at the same time, to trust completely in Christ’s love and mercy. Humility without hope would be despair; hope without humility, pride.
  • A word given to him: an experienced monk, after fifteen years of struggle. It is not an exercise to be imitated without guidance, but a light on Christian humility.

04 Love for enemies

For Saint Silouan, love for enemies is not a perfection reserved for a few: it is the criterion of true life in the Holy Spirit. Whoever does not love his enemies has not yet known the Lord as he is.

He who does not love his enemies does not have the grace of God in him.Saint Silouan, Writings
The Holy Spirit teaches us to love our enemies in such a way that the soul pities them as if they were its own children.Saint Silouan, Writings

He reflects on those who wish hellfire on their enemies, or on the enemies of the Church: if God gave you a good place in paradise and you saw in the fire the one whose torment you desired, would you not pity him? Christ prayed for those who crucified him (Luke 23:34), and Saint Stephen for those who stoned him. “And we, if we want to keep grace, must pray for our enemies.”

05 Prayer for the whole world

Whoever has tasted God’s love desires it for all. Every day, with tears, Saint Silouan prayed that all peoples might come to know the Lord; his disciple wrote that all his life he “shed the blood of his heart” in prayer for his enemies and for the whole world.

Lord, grant me to shed tears for myself and for the whole universe, that the peoples may know you and live with you for ever.Saint Silouan, Writings
Blessed is the soul that loves its brother, for “our brother is our life”.Saint Silouan, Writings

This prayer is born of longing for God. His writings are full of this yearning for the Lord: the soul that has known him finds rest nowhere else, like Adam weeping for the paradise he had lost.

Where are you, my Light, where are you, my joy? Your footsteps are fragrant in my soul, but you are not here, and my soul yearns for you.Saint Silouan, Writings
