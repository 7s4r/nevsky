Confession in the Orthodox Church

01 What is confession?

Confession is the sacrament of repentance (in Greek metanoia, “a change of mind”). Believers acknowledge their sins before God, in the presence of the priest, and receive forgiveness. The Fathers call it a “second baptism”: it renews the grace received in baptism.

It is not a tribunal but a healing. The priest is not a judge but a witness, and it is Christ himself who forgives. As the prayer before confession says: “Behold, my child, Christ stands here invisibly and receives your confession.”

02 When to go to confession

  • Before communion: in the Russian tradition, people usually go to confession before receiving communion, especially if they do not receive it often. The priest may suggest a different rhythm.
  • During the fasts, above all Great Lent, a privileged time of repentance.
  • Whenever your conscience is burdened, without waiting.
  • Children begin going to confession at around seven; before that, they receive communion without confession.

At the parish, you can go to confession before or during the services, or at another time arranged with the priest through the contact form.

03 Preparing

  • Examination of conscience: take time to look at your life in the light of the Gospel, for example the Ten Commandments and the Beatitudes. Some people write their sins down so as not to forget anything.
  • Prayer: ask God for light to see your faults and strength to change. The evening prayers can help.
  • Reconciliation: as far as possible, ask forgiveness of those you have hurt, and forgive in turn (Matthew 5:23-24).
  • Before communion, one also prepares by fasting and the appointed prayers; the priest will tell you what is suitable.

04 How confession takes place

  1. The priest stands at a lectern on which the Cross and the Gospel are placed. He first reads prayers for those coming to confession.
  2. Each person comes forward in turn and simply names their sins, without justifying themselves, blaming others or going into needless detail. You may speak in French or Russian.
  3. The priest may ask a question, give advice or sometimes a penance (epitimia): not a punishment, but a remedy.
  4. You bow your head; the priest covers it with his stole (epitrachelion) and reads the prayer of absolution.
  5. You kiss the Cross and the Gospel and receive the priest’s blessing.
Repentance is the renewal of baptism.St John Climacus, The Ladder of Divine Ascent

05 Spiritual guidance

Beyond confession, the priest is available to talk, listen and pray with anyone who wishes: a question of faith, a trial, a bereavement, a decision to make. These meetings need not be linked to the sacrament; simply ask for an appointment.

In short

  • A sacrament of healing: it is Christ who forgives.
  • Go to confession regularly, usually before communion.
  • Prepare through examination of conscience, prayer and reconciliation.
  • Simply name your sins, receive advice and absolution.
  • The priest is also there to listen, outside confession.
Frequently asked questions

Questions about confession

Simply tell the priest as you come forward: he will guide you. It is often helpful to meet him beforehand to get acquainted and ask your questions.

There is no single rule. In the Russian tradition, people usually go before each communion; others keep a regular rhythm agreed with their spiritual father. What matters is sincerity, not routine.

The sacrament of confession is given to Orthodox faithful. If you are not Orthodox, the priest will gladly meet you, listen to you and pray for you.

God knows the heart: a sincere confession is not a perfect inventory. If a serious sin comes back to you later, mention it at your next confession.
