Orthodox fasting: a simple guide

01 Why fast?

Christ Himself fasted forty days in the desert, and He said of His disciples: “The days will come, when the bridegroom shall be taken from them, and then shall they fast” (Matthew 9:15). Since the Apostles, the Church has fasted.

Fasting is neither a punishment nor a diet. It is a way to free the heart: learning to say no to food, we learn to say no to the passions; going without a little, we become more attentive to God and to others. Fasting always goes with prayer and almsgiving.

Moreover when ye fast, be not, as the hypocrites, of a sad countenance. […] But thou, when thou fastest, anoint thine head, and wash thy face; that thou appear not unto men to fast, but unto thy Father which is in secret.Matthew 6:16–18

02 The fasting calendar

The Russian Church follows the Julian calendar; the dates below are given on the civil calendar.

  1. 7 weeks

    Great Lent

    The seven weeks before Pascha, including Holy Week. The longest and strictest fast; its dates change every year with Pascha.

  2. June – 11 July

    The Apostles’ Fast

    From the Monday after All Saints’ Sunday to the feast of Sts Peter and Paul on 12 July. Its length varies with the date of Pascha.

  3. 14 – 27 Aug

    The Dormition Fast

    Two weeks before the Dormition of the Theotokos (28 August), as strict as Great Lent, relaxed on the Transfiguration (19 August).

  4. 28 Nov – 6 Jan

    The Nativity Fast

    Forty days to prepare for Christmas. All about the Nativity Fast.

  5. Every week

    Wednesday and Friday

    Wednesday, in memory of the betrayal of Judas; Friday, in memory of Christ’s Passion.

  6. One day

    One-day fasts

    The eve of Theophany (18 January), the Beheading of St John the Baptist (11 September) and the Exaltation of the Cross (27 September).

Fast-free weeks

  • The Sviatki, from Christmas to the eve of Theophany (7–17 January).
  • The week after the Sunday of the Publican and the Pharisee.
  • Bright Week, after Pascha.
  • The week after Pentecost.
  • Cheesefare week, before Great Lent: only meat is avoided.

The parish calendar shows every fast day.

03 What one eats

In the Orthodox Church, fasting first of all means abstaining from meat, dairy and eggs. Depending on the day, the rule is stricter or lighter:

  • Ordinary Wednesdays and Fridays: no meat, dairy or eggs; fish only if a great feast falls on that day.
  • Nativity and Apostles’ fasts: fish allowed on Saturdays and Sundays and on certain feasts.
  • Great Lent and the Dormition Fast: fish only on the Annunciation, Palm Sunday and the Transfiguration; oil and wine on Saturdays and Sundays.

According to one’s strength. These rules come from the Typikon, written for monasteries. The sick, pregnant or nursing women, children, the elderly, those doing hard work or travelling fast according to their strength, or are dispensed. The right measure is found with the priest.

04 Fasting before communion

Before communion, one neither eats nor drinks from midnight on the eve of the Liturgy: this is the Eucharistic fast. It goes with reading the prayers before communion and, according to the practice of the Russian Church, confession.

For those who do not usually keep the fast days, the priest may ask for a few days of fasting before communion. Here too, talk to him.

05 Lenten meal ideas

Lenten cooking can be simple, good and joyful. A few ideas, from Russia and from here in Béarn:

  • Soups: Lenten borscht, lentil soup, mushroom soup, or a Béarn garbure without meat, with cabbage and beans.
  • Main dishes: buckwheat kasha with onions and mushrooms, baked potatoes with sauerkraut, potato vareniki, rice with vegetables, pasta with tomato sauce, ratatouille.
  • Salads: Russian vinegret (beetroot, potatoes, carrots, pickles, oil), bean salad, hummus.
  • Fish days: a Pyrenean river trout, baked salmon, fish soup (ukha).
  • Sweets: compotes, dried fruit and nuts, honey, egg-free gingerbread, red-berry kissel.

Fasting also invites simplicity: replacing a rich dish with an elaborate plant-based equivalent keeps the letter of the fast, but not quite its spirit.

06 The spirit of fasting

Fasting does not make us better than others. St Paul warns both those who fast and those who do not:

Let not him that eateth despise him that eateth not; and let not him which eateth not judge him that eateth.Romans 14:3
  • No judging: we do not watch other people’s plates.
  • Love comes first: as a guest, one may simply accept what is served rather than hurt one’s host.
  • A fast of the whole person: fasting also from harsh words, anger and screens, to make room for prayer.
