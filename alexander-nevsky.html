Saint Alexander Nevsky

“God is not in might, but in truth.”Saint Alexander Nevsky, according to his Life

Prince of Novgorod and then grand prince of Vladimir, Saint Alexander Nevsky defended Russia in the west and protected his people before the Golden Horde. He died a monk, with the name Alexis. He is the patron saint of our church in Pau.Prince, defender of Russia, a monk at the end of his life, and patron saint of our church.

Saint Alexander Nevsky, patron of our church

01 A life in dates

Alexander was the son of Prince Yaroslav Vsevolodovich. He grew up in a divided Russia, at the time when the Mongol invasion was ravaging the land. While still very young he was given charge of Novgorod, the great merchant city of the North.

  1. 1221

    Birth

    In Pereslavl-Zalessky, on 13 May according to tradition.

  2. 1240

    The Battle of the Neva

    On 15 July he repels a Swedish landing on the banks of the Neva. The people call him “Nevsky”, “of the Neva”.

  3. 1242

    The Battle on the Ice

    On 5 April, on the ice of Lake Peipus, he halts the knights of the Livonian Order.

  4. 1252

    Grand Prince of Vladimir

    He rules north-eastern Russia and travels several times to the Horde to protect his people.

  5. 1263

    The monk Alexis

    Falling ill on his way back from the Horde, he receives the great schema with the name Alexis and dies at Gorodets on 14 November. He is buried in Vladimir on 23 November.

  6. 1547

    Canonization

    The Council of Moscow, under Metropolitan Macarius, proclaims him a saint of the Russian Church.

  7. 1724

    In Saint Petersburg

    Peter the Great has his relics transferred on 30 August to the Alexander Nevsky Monastery, later the Lavra.

02 “God is not in might, but in truth”

Before the Battle of the Neva, when his army was small, his Life relates that he prayed for a long time in the Cathedral of Holy Wisdom in Novgorod, and then encouraged his companions with words that became famous: “God is not in might, but in truth.”

It was not military victory that made him a saint. The Church venerates him as a right-believing prince (in Russian blagoverny): a man of power who put his strength at the service of justice, of his people’s faith and of peace.

03 Between the West and the Horde

Saint Alexander lived at a time when Russia was threatened on two sides. In the west he defended the Orthodox faith of his people, and his Life relates that he refused the proposals of religious submission that came from Rome. In the east, before the all-powerful Golden Horde, he chose the way of humility and negotiation rather than a war that was lost from the start.

He travelled to the khan several times, on long and dangerous journeys, to obtain relief for his people. It was on the way back from his last journey that he fell ill and died.

What the Church remembers of him

  • Courage in the service of his people, without seeking glory.
  • Humility: knowing how to bow before someone stronger to protect the weak.
  • Faithfulness to the Orthodox faith.
  • Prayer: before every decision, and to the end of his life as a monk.

04 The sun of the Russian land

When news of his death reached Vladimir, Metropolitan Cyril announced it to the people gathered in the cathedral with words that are still quoted today:

My dear children, know that the sun of the land of Suzdal has set.Metropolitan Cyril, according to the Life of Saint Alexander; tradition also says “the sun of the Russian land”

His Life, written soon after his death by a witness, relates that at his burial the saint himself stretched out his hand to receive the prayer of absolution. From the very beginning the faithful venerated him as a saint.

05 The patron saint of our church

Saint Alexander Nevsky is one of the best-loved Russian saints, and the patron of many churches built by Russian communities abroad in the nineteenth century, from Paris to Copenhagen.

So it is with our church in Pau, founded in 1867 for the Russian colony that came to enjoy the mild climate of Béarn. See the history of the church on the home page.

We celebrate our patron saint twice a year: on 6 December (23 November in the Julian calendar), the parish’s patronal feast, and on 12 September (30 August), the translation of his relics. Both feasts appear in the parish calendar.
