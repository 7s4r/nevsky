Baptism in the Orthodox Church

01 What is baptism?

Baptism is a new birth. Immersed three times in the water in the name of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit, the newly baptized dies with Christ and rises with him (Romans 6:3-4). Their sins are forgiven and they become a member of the Body of Christ, the Church.

In the Orthodox Church, baptism is immediately followed by chrismation, the anointing with holy chrism that gives the Holy Spirit, and then by first communion. One thus enters the Church in a single service. Baptism is received only once: “I confess one baptism for the remission of sins” (the Creed).

02 Asking for baptism

  • Get in touch with the priest as early as possible, through the contact form or after a service. He will meet you to get to know you and agree on the preparation and the date.
  • For a child, the parents make the request. The priest will ask them and the godparents to commit to raising the child in the Orthodox faith: praying with them, bringing them to church and to communion.
  • For a teenager or an adult, baptism is preceded by a time of preparation, the catechumenate: meetings with the priest, taking part in the services and discovering the faith, usually over several months.
  • If you were baptized in another Church, speak to the priest: depending on the case, you may be received into the Orthodox Church by chrismation or by profession of faith.

03 Godparents

Every person baptized has at least one godparent; traditionally a godfather for a boy and a godmother for a girl, and often both. During the service, the godparent answers on behalf of a small child and recites the Creed.

  • A godparent must be Orthodox, practising and old enough to take on this commitment.
  • Parents cannot be godparents of their own child.
  • The role does not end on the day of baptism: the godparent prays for their godchild all their life and accompanies them in the faith.

04 Preparing

  • Meetings with the priest: he explains the meaning of baptism and the order of the service, and answers your questions.
  • The Creed (the Symbol of Faith): the godparent, or the adult being baptized, recites it during the service. It is good to learn it and understand it.
  • Prayer: pray every day, for example with the morning and evening prayers.
  • Confession and communion: godparents are encouraged to go to confession and receive communion shortly before the baptism.

05 What to bring

  • A baptismal cross on a chain or cord, which the newly baptized will wear all their life.
  • A white garment (baptismal robe or gown), the sign of purity restored.
  • A large towel and a sheet for coming out of the water; for a baby, a change of clothes.
  • Candles for the godparents.

The priest will confirm what you need to bring.

06 How the service unfolds

The service lasts about an hour and has several parts:

  1. At the entrance of the church, the priest prays over the person to be baptized (prayers of exorcism). Facing west, the candidate — or the godparent for a child — renounces Satan; then, facing east, unites themselves to Christ and recites the Creed.
  2. The blessing of the water in the font and of the oil; the priest anoints the candidate with oil.
  3. The baptism: the priest immerses the candidate three times, saying “The servant of God N. is baptized in the name of the Father, amen, and of the Son, amen, and of the Holy Spirit, amen.” The newly baptized is then clothed in the white garment and receives the cross.
  4. Chrismation: the priest anoints the forehead, eyes, nostrils, lips, ears, chest, hands and feet with holy chrism, saying “The seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit.”
  5. The procession: the priest, the newly baptized and the godparents walk three times around the font with lighted candles, singing “As many as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ. Alleluia!”
  6. The readings from the Epistle (Romans 6:3-11) and the Gospel (Matthew 28:16-20).
  7. The tonsure: the priest cuts a few locks of hair in the form of a cross, the first offering of the newly baptized to God.

07 After baptism

The newly baptized receives communion the same day or at the following Liturgy, and then regularly: baptism is the beginning of a life in the Church. Each year they celebrate their patron saint (name day). The baptismal garment is carefully kept, and the cross is worn.

In short

  • Contact the priest well in advance.
  • At least one Orthodox godparent.
  • Learn the Creed.
  • Bring a cross, a white garment, a towel and candles.
  • About an hour: baptism, chrismation and procession.
